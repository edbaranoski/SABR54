# SABR54
This directory contains materials for my SABR 54 poster titled "Why Are Batters Evaluated by Plate Appearance and Pitchers by Inning?"  The title of the talk dates to my original submission and was too late to change, but the poster migrated into a discussion about how the statistical variance of various ERA predictors is a bigger factor than the stability of the underlying pitching paramters.  

There are so many factors in a pitcher's performance so it's hard to isolate individual factors from year to year. General consensus is the one year’s ERA is a poor estimate of the following year’s ERA.  Fielding Independent Pitching (FIP) performs much better and K%-BB% is better yet.  This was widely believed to be due to the underlying rates of the three true outcomes (HRs, Ks, and walks) being more constant from year to year than balls in play.  This talk shows that assumption is not the major reason.  A bigger factor is the variance of each individual component even when the underlying rate stats are held fixed from year-to-year.  

The approach I took was to freeze all the underlying statistics to create a hypothetical pitcher with the exact same underlying parameters from year to year (the same expected ERA, HR rate, K%, BB%, ground ball %, etc.).  I then take thousands of random statistical "seasons" using those same underlying parameters and compute the various pitching predictors (ERA, FIP, SIERA, etc.) to see see how much they vary if you did have an identical pitcher with identical defense, park factors, etc.  The expected variances can be easily computed from the underlying parameters, and it agrees with the sample "season" distributions.  

The findings basically show that ERA has a huge sample variance even assuming the identical underlying statistics. It also shows why some estimators have lower sample variances than others.  For example, K%- BB% has a higher correlation with ERA than FIP, largely because FIP's weighting of HRs greatly increases its sampling variance.
